Course Overview
This four-day short course provides practical foundations in geodesy, geospatial data and Earth observation mapping for Pacific Island contexts. Through lectures and hands-on exercises, participants will work with datums, heights, map projections and local geospatial data; explore airborne LiDAR processing and digital terrain model generation; undertake photogrammetric and drone mapping; and use Landsat and Sentinel-2 imagery for land, water and coastline mapping. Practical activities will use QGIS, Python, WebODM and relevant Digital Earth Pacific tools and data services, supported by selected examples of international disaster-response mapping, Earth observation applications and emerging data sources.

Rationale
The course responds to the need for stronger practical capability in geodesy, geospatial data management and Earth observation mapping among FNU students and emerging practitioners. These skills are increasingly important for producing reliable maps and elevation products, monitoring coastline movement and other coastal changes, and assessing the spatial impacts of cyclones, flooding and other disasters to support preparedness, response and recovery across Pacific Island countries and territories.

The course is deliberately practice-oriented. Participants will acquire and prepare local geospatial data, process airborne LiDAR, generate digital terrain models, create elevation models and orthoimages from drone imagery, and apply remote sensing workflows for classification, object detection and coastline extraction. Using accessible tools such as QGIS, Python, WebODM and relevant Digital Earth Pacific tools and data services will strengthen participants’ ability to transfer these methods to further study and locally relevant mapping applications.
Learning Objectives
•	Explain core geodetic and geospatial concepts, including datums, geoids, heights, map projections and common geospatial data types.
•	Acquire, prepare and visualise local geospatial data using QGIS.
•	Process airborne LiDAR data to identify ground and non-ground features and generate digital terrain models.
•	Apply photogrammetric workflows to drone imagery to produce three-dimensional reconstructions, digital elevation models and orthoimages using WebODM.
•	Use Landsat and Sentinel-2 imagery through Python-based workflows and relevant Digital Earth Pacific tools to classify land and water, monitor coastline change, and map disaster impacts for Pacific applications.

Delivery Approach
The course will be delivered through a structured mix of lectures, instructor-led demonstrations, laboratory-based practical sessions, daily recaps and quizzes, group exercises, presentations and course evaluation. Participants will work with prepared local and regional datasets to progressively develop skills in geospatial data preparation, LiDAR and drone-data processing, remote sensing classification, coastal change detection and disaster-impact mapping using QGIS, Python, WebODM and relevant Digital Earth Pacific tools and data services.

Expected Participants
The expected participants are approximately 24-30 Fiji National University students, primarily 300-level and postgraduate students in civil engineering and environmental sciences. The course is intended to support both academic learning and professional readiness by exposing students to applied EO workflows relevant to Pacific Island conditions.

Expected Outcomes
•	Participants demonstrate an improved understanding of geodesy, geospatial data and Earth observation mapping principles relevant to Pacific Island contexts.
•	Participants acquire, prepare, visualise and correctly project local geospatial data using QGIS.
•	Participants produce and interpret practical mapping outputs from airborne LiDAR and drone imagery, including filtered point clouds, digital terrain or elevation models, orthoimages and three-dimensional reconstructions.
•	Participants apply Landsat and Sentinel-2 imagery through Python-based workflows and relevant Digital Earth Pacific tools to classify land and water, monitor coastline change and map the spatial impacts of cyclones, flooding and other disasters.
•	Participants present and interpret locally relevant mapping results and identify how the methods can support coastal management, disaster preparedness, response and recovery in Pacific Island countries and territories.
